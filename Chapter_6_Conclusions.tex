\subsection*{Moment Equations for the Equal-Loss Two-Mode Coupler}

We consider two bosonic modes with annihilation operators 
$\hat a_{1}$ and $\hat a_{2}$, coupled through the passive
beam–splitter Hamiltonian
\[
\hat H = J(\hat a_{1}^{\dagger}\hat a_{2} 
+ \hat a_{2}^{\dagger}\hat a_{1}),
\]
and subject to equal damping rates 
\[
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\kappa.
\]

The relevant complex Gaussian moments are defined as
\[
\begin{aligned}
	n_{1} &= \langle \hat a_{1}^{\dagger}\hat a_{1}\rangle , &
	n_{2} &= \langle \hat a_{2}^{\dagger}\hat a_{2}\rangle ,\\[4pt]
	m_{1} &= \langle \hat a_{1}\hat a_{1}\rangle , &
	m_{2} &= \langle \hat a_{2}\hat a_{2}\rangle ,\\[4pt]
	s     &= \langle \hat a_{1}\hat a_{2}\rangle , &
	c     &= \langle \hat a_{1}^{\dagger}\hat a_{2}\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the Heisenberg--Langevin equations (noise averages vanish for a
vacuum bath), one obtains the coupled first-order differential equations
\[
\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
		\dot n_{1} &= -\kappa\, n_{1} 
		+ iJ\,(c^{*}-c),\\[4pt]
		\dot n_{2} &= -\kappa\, n_{2} 
		+ iJ\,(c-c^{*}),\\[8pt]
		\dot m_{1} &= -\kappa\, m_{1} 
		- 2iJ\, s,\\[4pt]
		\dot m_{2} &= -\kappa\, m_{2} 
		- 2iJ\, s,\\[8pt]
		\dot s     &= -\kappa\, s 
		- iJ\,(m_{1}+m_{2}),\\[4pt]
		\dot c     &= -\kappa\, c 
		+ iJ\,(n_{2}-n_{1}).
\end{aligned}}
\]

The initial conditions used throughout this work are
\[
\begin{aligned}
	n_{1}(0)=n_{10},\qquad &n_{2}(0)=n_{20},\\[4pt]
	m_{1}(0)=m_{10},\qquad &m_{2}(0)=m_{20},\\[4pt]
	s(0)=0,\qquad &c(0)=0,
\end{aligned}
\]
corresponding to uncorrelated input Gaussian states.

\subsection*{Analytic Solution Under Equal Loss}

The system above admits a closed-form solution when 
$\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\kappa$.
Define the constants
\[
C_{1}=n_{1}(0)+\tfrac12,\qquad 
C_{2}=n_{2}(0)+\tfrac12,
\]
\[
D_{1}=m_{1}(0),\qquad 
D_{2}=m_{2}(0).
\]

Then the exact solutions for all Gaussian moments are
\[
\begin{aligned}
	n_{1}(t) &= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[(C_{1}+C_{2}-1)
	+(C_{1}-C_{2})\cos(2Jt)\Big],\\[8pt]
	n_{2}(t) &= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[(C_{1}+C_{2}-1)
	-(C_{1}-C_{2})\cos(2Jt)\Big],\\[10pt]
	c(t) &= +\frac{i\,e^{-\kappa t}}{2}(C_{2}-C_{1})\sin(2Jt),\\[10pt]

	+(D_{2}-D_{1})\Big],\\[10pt]
	s(t) &= -\frac{i\,e^{-\kappa t}}{2}(D_{1}+D_{2})\sin(2Jt).
\end{aligned}
\]

\section{physical system configuration}
our system consist of two identical single mode waveguides which are placed parallel to each other in a close distance  such that their evanscent feild overlap and enable coupling between them.The optical modes in each waveguide are represented by creation and anhilation operator $a_1$ and $a_2$ where $a_1$ represents optical mode in waveguide $a_1$ and $a_2$ represents optical modein waveguide $a_2$.Each waveguide mode act as  a bosonic harmonic oscillator.
\subsection{system Hamiltonian}
The hamiltonian for our system is given by
\begin{equation}
	H = \hbar \omega \left(a_1^{\dagger} a_1 + a_2^{\dagger} a_2\right)
	+ \hbar J \left(a_1^{\dagger} a_2 + a_2^{\dagger} a_1\right).
\end{equation}

Here, $a_1$ and $a_2$ are the bosonic field operators for the optical modes in each waveguide, satisfying the commutation relations
$[a_1, a_1^{\dagger}] = 1$ and $[a_2, a_2^{\dagger}] = 1$.

the symbol $\omega$ is angular frequency of the each waveguide mode and the parameter J in the second part of the hamiltonian represents the coupling strength between the two waveguides and its value depends on the seperation distance between the waveguides.

The hamiltonian consists of two main parts: 

\textbf{Free energy terms:} correspond to the free energy of the individual waveguide modes,
\[
\hbar \omega \left(a_1^{\dagger} a_1 + a_2^{\dagger} a_2\right).
\]

\textbf{Coupling terms:} account for the evanescent coupling between the two waveguide modes,
\[
\hbar J \left(a_1^{\dagger} a_2 + a_2^{\dagger} a_1\right).
\]
This term is responsible for the transfer of energy between the two waveguide modes and is the main focus of interest in this work.


we can determine how this system evolve with loss by quantum louiville equation.
\begin{equation}
	\dot{\rho} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H, \rho] + \mathcal{L}\rho
\end{equation}
this equation is know as quantum  louiville equation.it has two parts:

\textbf{The Commutator}:\[
\boldsymbol{[H,\rho]}
\]
which generates time evolution and is responsible for oscillatory exchange of energy and quantum correlation
between the waveguides due to J.


\textbf{Lindblad($L\rho$) term
}: deals with material loss, decoherence and decays due to interaction with the environment

\begin{align}
	\mathcal{L}\rho
	&= -k_1\left(a_1^\dagger a_1\rho + \rho a_1^\dagger a_1 - 2a_1\rho a_1^\dagger\right)
	\nonumber\\
	&\quad -k_2\left(a_2^\dagger a_2\rho + \rho a_2^\dagger a_2 - 2a_2\rho a_2^\dagger\right),
\end{align}

$k_1$ and $k_2$ represents the loss rates of modes $a$ and $b$ ,and $a$, $a^{\dagger}$ are the annihilation and creation operators for mode $a$, while $\rho$ is the density matrix.  $b$ and $b^{\dagger}$ are vice versa for mode $b$.

\section{Dynamical equations of the model system}

The time evolution of any system observable $O$ is obtained from
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle O\rangle
	= \mathrm{Tr}(O\dot{\rho})
	= -i\langle[O,H]\rangle + \langle \mathcal{L}^\dagger(O)\rangle .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Equations of motion for the moments}

Using

$\frac{d}{dt}\langle O\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(O\dot{\rho})$,

Equation number(3.2), the coupled equations of motion becomes:


\begin{align}
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle
	&= iJ\Big(\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle-\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle\Big)
	- k_1\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle, \\[6pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle
	&= iJ\Big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle-\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle\Big)
	- k_2\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle, \\[6pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle
	&= iJ\Big(\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle-\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle\Big)
	-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle, \\[6pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle
	&= iJ\Big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle-\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle\Big)
	-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle, \\[10pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_1 a_1\rangle
	&= -2iJ\langle a_1 a_2\rangle
	- k_1\langle a_1 a_1\rangle, \\[6pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_2 a_2\rangle
	&= -2iJ\langle a_1 a_2\rangle
	- k_2\langle a_2 a_2\rangle, \\[6pt]
	\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_1 a_2\rangle
	&= -iJ\Big(\langle a_1 a_1\rangle+\langle a_2 a_2\rangle\Big)
	-\frac{k_1+k_2}{2}\langle a_1 a_2\rangle.
\end{align}
so we have got seven differential equations,now we are moving towards the solutions of these equations


\subsection{analytical solutions of the moment equations}

For identical waveguides, we set
\begin{equation}
	k_1 = k_2 = \kappa .
\end{equation}
Without imposing any restriction on the initial correlations, the seven second-order
moments admit the following closed-form solutions:
\begin{align}
	\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle(t)
	&= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[
	\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0+\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0
	+ \big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\cos(2Jt)
	\nonumber\\
	&\qquad
	+ i\big(\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[8pt]
	%
	\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle(t)
	&= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[
	\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0+\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0
	- \big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\cos(2Jt)
	\nonumber\\
	&\qquad
	- i\big(\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[10pt]
	%
	\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle(t)
	&= e^{-\kappa t}\Big[
	\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\cos(2Jt)
	- \frac{i}{2}\big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[10pt]
	%
	\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle(t)
	&= e^{-\kappa t}\Big[
	\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle_0\cos(2Jt)
	+ \frac{i}{2}\big(\langle a_1^\dagger a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2^\dagger a_2\rangle_0\big)\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[10pt]
	%
	\langle a_1 a_1\rangle(t)
	&= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[
	\langle a_1 a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2 a_2\rangle_0
	+ \big(\langle a_1 a_1\rangle_0+\langle a_2 a_2\rangle_0\big)\cos(2Jt)
	\nonumber\\
	&\qquad
	- 2i\,\langle a_1 a_2\rangle_0\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[8pt]
	%
	\langle a_2 a_2\rangle(t)
	&= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[
	-\big(\langle a_1 a_1\rangle_0-\langle a_2 a_2\rangle_0\big)
	+ \big(\langle a_1 a_1\rangle_0+\langle a_2 a_2\rangle_0\big)\cos(2Jt)
	\nonumber\\
	&\qquad
	- 2i\,\langle a_1 a_2\rangle_0\sin(2Jt)
	\Big], \\[10pt]
	%
	\langle a_1 a_2\rangle(t)
	&= e^{-\kappa t}\Big[
	\langle a_1 a_2\rangle_0\cos(2Jt)
	- \frac{i}{2}\big(\langle a_1 a_1\rangle_0+\langle a_2 a_2\rangle_0\big)\sin(2Jt)
	\Big].
\end{align}






Although seven coupled moment equations are obtained, they do not all correspond to
independent dynamical variables. In particular, the inter-mode coherence terms
$\langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle$ and $\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle$ are related by
Hermitian conjugation, i.e.,
$\langle a_2^\dagger a_1\rangle = \langle a_1^\dagger a_2\rangle^{*}$.
Therefore, only six independent moments are sufficient to fully describe the system
dynamics, while the remaining coherence follows directly from this relation.

\[

\[
D_{1}=\langle a_{1}a_{1}\rangle_{0},
\qquad
D_{2}=\langle a_{2}a_{2}\rangle_{0}.
\]

	n_{1}(t) &= \frac{e^{-\kappa t}}{2}\Big[(C_{1}+C_{2}-1)
	+(C_{1}-C_{2})\cos(2Jt)\Big],\\[8pt]

	-(C_{1}-C_{2})\cos(2Jt)\Big],\\[10pt]
	c(t) &= +\frac{i\,e^{-\kappa t}}{2}(C_{2}-C_{1})\sin(2Jt),\\[10pt]
