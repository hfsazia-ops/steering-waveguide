\chapter{Results and discussion}
In this chapter,we studied the dynamical behaviour of the Gaussian Quantum Steering and Gaussian Quantum Entanglement in a coupled lossy waveguide system using the Covariance matrix formalism.This analysis was conducted by initially considering a general reference case where the nonclassicality depth, purity and loss parameters were held constant and then by considering the action of each of these parameters separately. we investigated the effect of these parameters by keeping the coupling term  $J$  constant  i,e 1 and by plotting Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Steering against $ \frac{Jt}{\pi} $,where $J$ =1.We also studied a special kind of guassian states called squeezed states and analyzed the effect of squeezing on Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Steering.with the same consideration i,e $J$ =1 and plotting Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Steering against $ \frac{Jt}{\pi} $.
we further investigated effect of coupling term  $J$ on Quantum Entanglement and  Quantum Steering by keeping $\kappa$
constant i,e 1, and plotted Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Steering against $\kappa t$
 to see the effect.

\section{Time Evolution of Gaussian Steering and Entanglement}

This section discusses the analysis of the general time dynamics of the Gaussian Quantum Steering and Gaussian Entanglement in a coupled lossy waveguide system.The nonclassical depth $\tau$, purity $\mu$, and loss parameter $\kappa$ are kept fixed, and time evolution dynamics of Entanglement and Steering is investigated.
The evolution of the logarithmic negativity $E_{\mathcal{N}}$ and the Gaussian steering $\mathcal{S}$ with the normalized propagation time is illustrated by Fig 4.1.The quantum correlations are also periodic, which means the coherent exchange between quantum correlations of the two interacting waveguide modes. The maxima and minima of Gaussian steering and entanglement are situated in the same propagation lengths meaning that both the correlations can be related to the same underlying mode -coupling dynamics that is brought about by the evanescent interaction between the waveguides.
It is also noted that the value of Gaussian steering is never greater than that of the Gaussian entanglement with time. This is agreeing with this fact of quantum correlations: steering is a more limited, but more effective form of nonclassicality than entanglement. As time passes, the steering and entanglement sluggishly reduce owing to the energy loss of the waveguides which signifies that the dissipation harms the conservation of the quantum correlations.

\section{Effect of Nonclassical Depth $\tau$}

In this section, we consider the effects of non classicality, referred to as nonclassicality depth, $\tau$ on Gaussian quantum steering and Gaussian Quantum entanglement,we have kept the purity $\mu$ and the loss parameter $\kappa$ fixed in this analysis.

Figure 4.2 indicates that the greater the nonclassical depth the stronger becomes both Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Gaussian steering. The larger the $\tau$ the larger the peaks, the larger the Quantum correlations as they travel.This is to imply that the greater the nonclassicality of the initial Gaussian state is, the greater the ability it has to generate and maintain quantum correlations in the coupled waveguide system.

In addition, the Gaussian steering responds more to variation in the nonclassical depth in comparison to entanglement. This increased sensitivity is an indication that steering is more strict that entanglement requires stronger quadrature correlations.

\section{Effect of coupling strength}
Finally, In this section we investigate the effect of the coupling strength $J$ on Gaussian quantum entanglement and Gaussian steering, we kept the loss parameter $\kappa$ fixed and set to unity in this analysis.The quantum correlations are plotted versus the scaled parameter $\kappa t$, which represents the propagation time in the presence of loss. By fixing $\kappa$=1, we have observed variations in the dynamical behavior of Quantum entanglement and Quantum steering by changing the coupling strength $J$.
As the coupling becomes stronger, both Gaussian entanglement and Gaussian steering amplitude maximum values are significantly attained. This demonstrates that a higher coupling assists in the generation and transport of quantum correlations across the waveguides that partially compensates the adverse impact of loss. Moreover, the stronger the coupling, the stronger the oscillations, which means that there is a bigger rate of coherent exchange of energy between the coupled modes.
The findings indicate that better coupling enhances the generation of quantum correlations and both entanglement and steering peaks are larger. The increase in the level of coupling facilitates easier movement of photons between the waveguides hence increasing the strength of the correlations between modes despite some level of loss. In the weaker connection, the correlations are narrow and fade away more rapidly with the wave transmission. The findings reveal that the enhancement of the formation of quantum correlations is achieved by stronger coupling, which provides higher peaks of both Gaussian entanglement and Gaussian steering. The increased coupling causes the photons to travel between the waveguides with lesser effort and hence the correlations between the modes become stronger even when a certain amount of loss is experienced. Conversely, when the coupling strengths are weaker the correlations generated are finite and they decay faster as they propagate.
It is also observed that,regardless of the strength of the coupling between the two systems, Gaussian entanglement is always bigger than Gaussian steering.

\section{Summary}
The key findings of this chapter can be summarized as follows:
This chapter has included an in-depth exploration of the dynamical performance of Gaussian quantum entanglement and Gaussian quantum steering within a coupled lossy waveguide system by the covariance matrix framework. This analysis has been done through the systematic investigation of the role of important physical parameters that determine the dynamics of the system.
First of all we have learned the overall time evolution of entanglement and steering by setting the nonclassical depth, purity and loss parameters fixed. The experiment found an oscillatory behavior of both quantum correlations as functions of the normalized propagation time, and was due to the coherent mode-coupling interaction between the waveguides. It was found that maxima and minima in entanglement as well as steering take identical propagation lengths which validated their physical origins. As the process continued, the Gaussian steering was always smaller than the Gaussian entanglement, as it was known in the hierarchy of quantum correlations.
After that, the influence of nonclassical depth was analyzed. It was revealed that the increase in nonclassical depth positively affected the Gaussian entanglement and steering, and steering was more sensitive to nonclassicality changes. This shows the more high demands of steering, which have high quadrature correlation requirements compared to entanglement.
The effect of purity by the state was then examined. The purity decrease resulted in a major degradation of the entanglement and steering, thus showing the susceptibility of the two to noise and decoherence. This establishes that the only way to maintain quantum correlations in real systems is to ensure that the systems are higher purified.
The effects of the loss was also examined. An increment in the loss parameter led to a sharp reduction of the quantum correlations i,e quantum entanglement and quantum steering.
We then studied the role of squeezing with reference to squeezed Gaussian states. Squeezing in the absence of its loss has high entanglement and steering due to the increased quadrature correlations. Both of the correlations decreases by introducting loss.Although loss decreases quantum correlations but it doesnot vanish them , they are correlated even with effect of loss.
Lastly, the influence of the strength of coupling was investigated by holding the loss parameter constant. It was discovered that high coupling increases evanscent coupling signifying more efficient transfer of photons,hence increases number of oscillation which  produce great improvement in the amplitudes and persistence of entanglement and steering.which even compensate the effect of loss parameter which is kept 1.however,Steering was always less than entanglement which proves the hierarchy.
On the whole, this chapter confirms that the nonclassicality, purity, loss, squeezing, and the strength of the coupling in coupled waveguide systems have a strong impact on the Gaussian quantum entanglement and steering.Both Entanglement and steerinf are found to be robust and steering is less than entanglement for all values which proves the quantum hierarchy.
