\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chp5}

In this thesis, the dynamical behaviour of Guassian Quantum Steering and Guassian Quantum Entanglement have been investigated in a coupled lossy waveguide stytem using the covariance matrix formalism within the framework of continious variable quantum optics.The reason behind the study is that it is important to get the insights on the impact of realistic dissipation and system parameters on the persistence and utility of quantum correlations in integrated photonic platforms.

The analysis has revealed that, quantum entanglement and quantum steering have oscillatory behaviour with respect to propagation time which is explained by coherent transfer of energy between the coupled waveguide modes. These oscillations are indicative of the underlying evanescent mechanism of coupling and is a conclusive evidence of interaction between coherent modes within the system. However, the loss of correlations between the two in the presence of optical loss is what causes a gradual decay of both of the correlations, which in turn demonstrates the inevitable effect of dissipation in realistic photonic devices.

One of the key findings of the given work is that the hierarchical character of quantum correlations indeed exists: quantum steering is seen to be more vulnerable than quantum entanglement in the same physical conditions and is less in magnitude than entanglement.This behaviour is seen in all regimes of parameters studied and in total accord with the theoretical hierarchy between Quantum Entanglement and Quantum Steering.

Systematic study of the quantum correlation with significant physical parameters was done. The increase of the nonclassical depth and purity is discovered to increase entanglement and steering, and thus increase their resistance to loss. On the other hand, an increased loss coefficient increases the rate of quantum correlation degradation. The coupling parameter varies the rate of exchange of quantum correlations between the modes, it compensates the effect of loss parameter which is kept as 1 for this special case only.

The study of squeezed Gaussian states goes further to indicate that, whereas ideal lossless systems can sustain and strong quantum correlations, realistic loss ,although decreases quantum correlations but entanglement and steering are still robust even in the presence of loss.

To sum up, this paper has proved that useful amounts of Gaussian quantum correlations can be maintained in integrated waveguide systems with dissipation that cannot be avoided, offering important information about their practical constraints and abilities. The results provide a design and optimization rule of the photonic quantum devices, and in instances where asymmetric quantum resources are required, including but not limited to one-sided device-independent quantum key distribution, where quantum steering is key.
